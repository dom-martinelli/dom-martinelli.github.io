the review (Computers in Biology and Medicine, 2022)
question

Generative models can propose thousands of new drug-like molecules, but most are hard to synthesise and hard to compare. Which model families actually deliver, and how do you tell?

planned
  • a small open run: a SELFIES-based generator trained on a ChEMBL target set, scored against the MOSES and GuacaMol benchmark metrics, with the generated molecules drawn on the same chemical-space map as the wakefulness-drug project.
  • industry generative work (multi-objective reinforcement learning on thousands of molecules) is proprietary and is described here, not shown.
what came out
  • the review covered 87 studies: six model families (LSTM and GRU RNNs, VAEs, GANs, adversarial autoencoders, evolutionary algorithms) and eight recurring problems, led by poor synthesisability, thin assay data and no agreed way to compare models.
