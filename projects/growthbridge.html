A family-facing growth chart

Parents rarely see their child's growth chart between checkups. This demo app lets a family view the growth history stored in their health record and add measurements taken at home.

Live demoCode on GitHub

The question

What would it look like for families to see and understand their child's growth data from the health record, between doctor visits?

Why it matters

Growth problems are often caught late because measurements are only reviewed at scheduled visits. Giving families a clear view of the trend, with conservative flags when something looks off, could prompt earlier conversations with their pediatrician.

What I did

  • Built a patient-facing SMART on FHIR web app that reads patient and measurement data from the Epic sandbox, read-only.
  • Drew a combined interactive height and weight chart with details for each measurement date.
  • Added growth velocity, a midparental target height from parents' heights, corrected age for children born early, and a flag when a height z-score falls below -2.
  • Let families enter home measurements, stored only in their own browser.
  • Created mock scenarios, including a Turner syndrome teaching case, and deployed the app to GitHub Pages.

What I found

  • A small browser-only app can handle the full Epic sandbox launch flow and present growth data in a family-friendly way without any backend.

Limits

  • A demonstration, not a clinical product: it does not use a validated CDC or WHO percentile engine and does not write data back to the record.
  • Not yet tested with a real Epic sandbox client, and has no unit tests or accessibility review yet.
  • Home measurements live in the browser only, and the midparental height formula still needs documented sources.
