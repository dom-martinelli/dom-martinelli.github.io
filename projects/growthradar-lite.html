Growth monitoring from the health record

A child who slowly drops off their growth curve can go unnoticed across many short clinic visits. This prototype pulls a child's measurements from an electronic health record, flags worrying changes, and drafts a summary for the pediatrician.

Live demoCode on GitHub

The question

Can a lightweight app read a child's growth data straight from an Epic health record and surface growth faltering a busy clinician might miss?

Why it matters

Growth faltering and short stature can signal underlying disease, but spotting them means comparing many measurements over years against reference charts, which is easy to skip in a short visit. Pulling that history automatically and flagging drops could help catch problems earlier.

What I did

  • Connected to the Epic FHIR sandbox using the SMART on FHIR login flow (with PKCE) to read patient, vitals, lab, medication, and condition data.
  • Placed height measurements on WHO (under 2 years) and CDC (2 years and up) reference percentiles and calculated height velocity between visits.
  • Flagged large percentile drops and heights at or below the 3rd percentile.
  • Collected optional family context such as parents' heights for a midparental target height.
  • Sent the structured growth history and flags to a language model through OpenRouter to draft a short clinical summary, using a key kept only in the browser session.
  • Included a synthetic sample patient so the app works without an Epic login.

What I found

  • Standard health record APIs provide enough structured growth data to run percentile tracking and velocity checks in the browser, without a backend.

Limits

  • Percentiles are approximated from a small built-in reference table for boys, not a validated LMS calculation.
  • Runs against the Epic sandbox and synthetic data only, not real patients.
  • The AI summary is for demonstration and is not a substitute for clinical judgment.
