question

How far do patients in the Stroke Belt travel for a hospital stay, and does that distance line up with the rural hospitals that closed and the states that declined Medicaid expansion?

what came out
  • Stroke Belt patients travel farther for a hospital stay: a median 10.7 miles from home against 7.3 in the median state outside the Belt, and 14% of stays are more than 30 miles away against 8%. Mississippi is the extreme at 27%. For a time-critical condition like stroke, that distance is part of the disparity.
  • Rural hospital closures track that distance. Across states, a higher closure rate since 2010 goes with a larger share of stays over 30 miles (Spearman ρ 0.41, p = 0.003). The Belt accounts for 48 of the country's 154 closures.
  • The Belt splits on Medicaid expansion. Arkansas and Louisiana, the region's early expanders (2014, 2016), had 2 closures between them; the six states that held out through the decade had 46, Tennessee alone 14. This is an association across a handful of states, not a causal estimate, but it matches the reimbursement story: expansion shrinks the uncompensated care that small rural hospitals absorb.
calls made
  • miles travelled as the headline, not 'left the home county'. Georgia has 159 small counties, so almost any trip crosses a line there and the county measure overstated it.
  • distance measured between ZIP centroids, patient home to hospital. rough at the scale of one trip, stable across two million of them.
  • closure and expansion results reported as associations across a few states. eight states cannot carry a causal claim, and the card says so.
  • arcs under 20 stays dropped from the map, and CMS itself suppresses cells under 11, so the thinnest rural flows are missing rather than zero.
