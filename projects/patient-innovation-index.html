question

Patients have shaped research as authors, funders and data owners since the HIV activists of the 1980s. Can that involvement be measured from the published papers, and does involved research reach further?

what came out
  • papers with two or more involvement signals sit at a median relative citation ratio 52% above a random PubMed sample (0.995 against 0.655; 655 papers against 16,161).
  • the gradient is monotonic: median RCR 0.79 at one signal, 0.98 at two, 1.53 at three or more.
  • matched on year and team size (coarsened exact matching, 654 pairs), the gap is +31%.
  • structure carries the effect and vocabulary does not. patients as authors, affiliates and data owners predict impact; papers that only call themselves patient-led show nothing.
  • the effect concentrates where organised patient research already exists. of 70 disease headings, three survive false-discovery correction: HIV, COVID-19 and pain.
  • inside the same journal the direction holds in 13 of 18 journals, median +32%, so journal prestige is not doing the work.
  • the index weights are my own judgment, so six other weighting schemes and thresholds were run. every one stays positive and significant, from +24% to +54%.
  • involved papers are not cited faster. early citation velocity runs lower for papers with one signal and only catches up at three or more.
calls made
  • corpus retrieved on involvement terms, chunked by year, not built around a disease. the object of study is a mode of production, and year chunks stop the pull favouring recent work.
  • three separately built control sets: stratified random, journal-matched, heading-matched. one random comparison leaves journal and disease area confounded with the exposure.
  • coarsened exact matching on year and team size, not propensity scores. bounds imbalance up front instead of checking balance afterwards.
  • relative citation ratio as the outcome, not raw counts. each paper is normalised to its own co-citation field baseline.
  • false-discovery correction on the 70 per-heading tests, none on the primary comparison. the primary test was specified in advance; the headings are a multiple-testing family.
what broke
  • case-folding bug: the scorer lowercased the text but kept capitalised patterns, so every acronym signal could never fire.
  • substring matching on organisation names produced false hits on unrelated institutions.
  • the collective-author signal was tagging research consortia as patient collectives, which mislabeled a headline signal in the draft.
  • once patterns went case-insensitive, a bare sample-size notation started matching results-section text.
  • a figure and its signal table disagree because they came from different pipeline runs. reconciling to one canonical run is open.
what i tried

wanted involvement scored from each paper's funding-statement text. the corpus carries PubMed grant lists only, so OpenAlex funder metadata stood in.
