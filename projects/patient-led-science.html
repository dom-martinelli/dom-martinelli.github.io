Do patients' ideas reach the research?

Long COVID patients documented the crash after effort months before researchers did; the field followed their paper, NIH adopted it, exercise trials mostly did not

question

When patients document an illness before researchers do, does the research and policy that follows take up what they found?

what i did
  • dated the first patient record and the first scientific record of 17 lingering symptoms, counting only records of symptoms lasting 4+ weeks, every earliest hit read by hand.
  • split 10,923 Long COVID papers by whether they cite the patients' survey and tested PEM mentions with a year-stratified Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio, in abstracts and in full text.
  • benchmarked the patients' survey against 67 early papers chosen by a rule written before any result was computed.
  • checked PEM's weight in NIH's RECOVER definition and whether that definition now carries PEM without the patients' paper.
  • tagged 182 exercise and rehabilitation trial registrations for whether they mention, screen for or exclude PEM.
what came out
  • patients documented 13 of 17 lingering symptoms first; post-exertional malaise (PEM) by 159 days, brain fog by 47.
  • papers citing the patients' survey mention PEM about 2.8 times as often. among 67 comparable early papers it ranks 2nd: papers with a similar audience passed PEM to 13 to 20 later papers, the patients' survey to 63.
  • NIH's RECOVER index gives PEM 7 points toward a threshold of 12, and papers citing that definition lean toward PEM even without citing the patients (odds ratio 4.15).
  • only 15 of 182 exercise trials screen for PEM, the symptom that makes exercise risky for these patients.
calls made
  • the comparison pool by a rule written in advance, not hand-picked rivals. a hand-picked rival can always be argued away.
  • shrunk estimates over raw odds ratios for the ranking. a small paper can top a raw ranking by chance.
  • a 4-week rule for what counts as lingering, set after a first pass and labelled as such, with the loose dates kept as a check.
  • full text alongside abstracts. abstracts carry about one in seven PEM mentions.
what broke
  • the patients' survey is the only pool paper whose own abstract names PEM, so part of its lead is topical. reported as part of the finding, not hidden.
  • a credit analysis (were patients' published hypotheses cited?) was pulled: its definition of 'studied' was too loose and three of the studies began before the patients wrote anything.
so what
  • the patients' knowledge reached the research definition faster than it reached the clinic. a trial designer or reviewer can check PEM screening as a concrete gap.
not shown
  • the manuscript is in draft with my advisor; numbers may change before submission
