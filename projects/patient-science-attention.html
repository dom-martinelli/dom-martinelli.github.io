whether the field picks up what patients documented themselves, measured on the citing literature

question

When patients run the study themselves, does the research field go on to pay attention to what they found?

what came out
  • papers citing the patient-led survey mention post-exertional malaise at a pooled odds ratio of 3.89, against 1.11 to 1.37 for three researcher-led and clinician-led comparison sources.
  • the effect survived a rebuild on a different symptom list and a different data pipeline. PEM came back at 2.83, and PEM and brain fog were the only two units clearing every comparison.
  • the broad hypothesis failed. patient-observable symptoms as a class never moved, and per-symptom growth does not differ by class. the signal lives in two named constructs, not in subjective symptoms generally.
  • median log gap across usable symptoms is negative, so most of the patient survey's vocabulary is not what its citers pick up.
  • about 2.5 years from patient documentation to PEM entering the official research index at one of its highest weights.
calls made
  • unit lists and the patient-observable versus clinician-measurable split frozen in code before any count was pulled. the split is a judgment call, and freezing it is the only thing that keeps it from following the results.
  • three comparison sources rather than one. a single clinician-led cohort cannot separate a patient-led effect from a hospital-heavy one, and the researcher-led self-report source is what settles that.
  • a rebuild on published symptom tables rather than defending the hand-built list. an independent frame and an independent pipeline is a cheaper answer than an argument about term selection.
  • the null reported rather than cut. the class-level contrast was the planned headline and it came back flat.
  • method structure copied from an existing attention study on protein structure prediction, which is where the citing-split design comes from.
what broke
  • mentions are not stance. a paper arguing a treatment is harmful counts as attention to that treatment, and nothing here measures agreement.
  • citing is a choice, so none of this is causal. the patient survey is the reference for the construct it is being scored on, and the comparison sources size that bias rather than remove it.
  • coverage is thin. only 25 of 166 survey symptoms clear ten mentions among citers, and the survey's own patient language barely appears in abstracts.
  • two comparison sources were added after a preview of the first result. documented as post hoc, not pre-registered.
  • one rater, own term lists, terms that collide across fields.
what i tried

tried a yearly line of treatment papers co-mentioning the patient-named construct. numerators ran zero to four a year, so the line was noise and it was pooled into periods instead.

not shown
  • the parallel case in the older disease is parked, not dropped, pending a scope decision.
  • figures and the frozen protocol live with the code, not here.
