What a dose actually does, with two sliders

a teaching demo of the one-compartment model, for anyone who has seen the equation and not the curve: drag dose and clearance, watch which one changes the shape

skills
pharmacokinetic modelling · interactive UI design · explaining a model by letting someone break it
tools
  • Python
  • Streamlit
  • NumPy
  • matplotlib
data
no dataset. acetaminophen-like literature parameters as the starting point: 1000 mg, F = 0.86, CL = 21 L/h, Vd = 50 L
status
finished
question

Everyone meets C(t) = (F x Dose / Vd) e^(-kt) as an equation. What does each parameter actually do to the curve a patient lives with?

what i did
  • built a one-compartment model with instant distribution in Streamlit, with sliders for dose, bioavailability, clearance and volume of distribution.
  • wrote the page as a walkthrough rather than a tool: each slider arrives with the part of the equation it controls.
  • plotted the two parameters side by side to show that they do different kinds of thing to the curve.
planned
  • a one- or two-compartment model in the browser: pick a drug and a metabolizer phenotype, drag the dose and interval, and watch the concentration curve move against the therapeutic window.
  • the PharmGKB pathway for the same drug drawn beside it, so the enzyme the slider changes is visible in the metabolic route.
  • starting pair: amitriptyline and CYP2D6/CYP2C19, where CPIC gives explicit dose adjustments.
what came out
  • dose scales the curve and leaves its shape alone. Doubling it doubles every concentration and changes no timing.
  • clearance changes the shape. At the acetaminophen-like default the half-life is 1.7 hours; halving clearance stretches it to 3.3 and doubling it compresses it to 0.8.
  • that difference is the whole intuition behind why a metabolizer phenotype matters more than a dose adjustment in some cases, and it is visible in one drag.
calls made
  • one compartment with instant absorption rather than a two-compartment model with an absorption phase. The point is which parameter does what, and a second compartment adds terms without adding that insight.
  • literature-ish acetaminophen parameters rather than a fitted dataset. Nothing here is a claim about a real patient.
what i tried

this card used to describe a pharmacogenomic simulator with phenotype sliders and PharmGKB pathways drawn beside the curve. That was a plan, not a thing, and the notebook behind it is a Streamlit teaching demo from April. Rewritten to describe what exists. The plan is still a good one and is listed as not shown.

so what
  • a slider teaches an exponential better than an equation does, because the reader supplies the counterfactual themselves.
  • this is the scaffold the pharmacogenomic version would sit on. Swap the clearance slider for a CYP2D6 phenotype and the same curve becomes a clinical argument.
not shown
  • metabolizer phenotypes. The CYP2D6 and CYP2C19 version described in the earlier plan for this card was never built, and the 271 PharmGKB pathway files sit unused.
  • any real patient data. This is a model with typed-in parameters.
