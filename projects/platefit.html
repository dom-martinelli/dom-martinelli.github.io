question

Can restaurant menu items be ranked transparently by how well they fit the way someone on a GLP-1 medication has to eat?

calls made
  • a rubric, not a model. every score has to be explainable to someone deciding what to eat, and there is no labelled outcome to train on.
  • fried or breaded takes a categorical penalty that also caps the tier. a fried item can look moderate on the label and still trigger nausea.
  • missing nutrition fields pull the score toward a neutral 50, rather than renormalising over whatever is present. otherwise one field carries an item into the top tier on almost no data.
  • carbohydrates accepted but not scored. a direct carb penalty would punish high-carb, high-fibre items that suit the pattern.
  • no calorie floor. a small, protein-dense item is exactly what this eating pattern calls for.
what broke
  • the added-sugar axis cannot be scored as designed: US chains publish total sugars only, so the field carries an upper bound and the compromise is documented on every item.
  • part of the dataset had to be derived from per-ingredient values or comparable items, then flagged as estimated.
  • the macro-consistency check fails on near-zero-calorie drinks, where calories-from-macros is meaningless.
  • refined starch slips through: white rice or white bread carries no added sugar and is only caught indirectly.
not shown
  • thresholds are guidance-level, not trial-backed, so this is a ranking aid and not a clinical instrument
  • not deployed yet, so there is no link
