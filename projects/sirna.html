question

Can a model predict how well a chemically modified siRNA silences its target from sequence and modification alone?

calls made
  • three model families side by side rather than tuning one architecture. on a task nobody had modelled, which family fits is itself the finding.
  • ROC and precision-recall AUC, not accuracy. classes are unbalanced at every threshold, and a falsely optimistic call is the expensive mistake here.
  • the whole experiment repeated at three inhibition cutoffs. published definitions of an effective siRNA disagree with each other.
  • one-hot encoding of base and modification at each position, not cheminformatics descriptors of each modification. enough for a proof of concept, at the cost that a genuinely new chemistry cannot be scored without retraining.
what broke
  • the validation sequences the methods pointed to were missing from the supplement at first submission. a reviewer caught it.
  • the supplementary spreadsheet still carried an earlier set of activity cutoffs, contradicting the manuscript. reviewer caught that too.
  • reading the learned weights needs to account for how modifications co-occur. a high weight on one stabilising modification partly stands in for the others that travel with it.
what i tried

ran the classifiers at a 90 percent inhibition cutoff as well. too few sequences clear that bar, so the classes go too lopsided to learn from.
