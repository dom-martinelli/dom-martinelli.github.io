A codebook for what medical graders actually disagree about

not whether a checklist line is debatable, but what the disagreement is about: seven types, built from 79 hand-read lines

skills

tools

data
79 example-level checklist lines across 56 conversations, every one hand-read · a pool of 1,186 candidate lines awaiting labelling
status
in progress
question

A benchmark's checklist lines get scored by physicians who often disagree. A regex can say a line looks debatable. What it cannot say is what two clinicians would be arguing about, which is the thing that decides whether the disagreement is a flaw or the point.

what i did
  • rejected the earlier framing. A 22 November run asked whether a line was debatable and answered with a regex, which measures wording rather than disagreement.
  • reframed the question so it can be read off a single line: when a reasonable clinician would score this line the other way, what is the disagreement about?
  • built seven types naming a kind of disagreement, plus an exit code and a residual, from 79 lines read by hand.
  • wrote a master tie-break, a counterfactual test, so a line that fits two types has a stated rule rather than a coder's preference.
what came out
  • the useful question is not how much graders disagree but what about. Disagreement over a clinical fact, over how much to hedge, and over who the patient is are three different problems with three different fixes.
  • a property that can be read off a single line is labelable at scale. A property that needs the whole conversation is not, which is what makes the reframing the substantive move rather than a wording change.
calls made
  • one line as the unit of analysis, because it is the unit the benchmark actually scores.
  • holding the codebook at draft until it is approved, rather than labelling 1,186 lines against a rule set that may still move.
what i tried

the first version asked whether a line was debatable, which a regex will happily answer and which tells you about adjectives rather than about medicine.

so what
  • published medical AI benchmarks report agreement rates and not what the disagreements are made of, so a 44.6% disagreement rate on one category reads as noise when it may be a category that is genuinely contested.
  • a codebook with a stated tie-break is the difference between a label set another person can reproduce and one that encodes a single reader's intuition.
not shown
  • the labelling. 1,186 candidate lines are identified and none are labelled with this codebook yet.
  • no second coder and therefore no reliability statistic. Until there is one, the types are a proposal.
