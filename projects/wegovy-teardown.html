Wegovy vs. Zepbound: a commercial teardown

where a brand spends to defend share when a better-performing rival launches: Wegovy moved 28.7% of its physician spend to cardiologists in one year

skills
competitive positioning · payer and pricing analysis · lifecycle and exclusivity mapping · reading a label as a commercial asset
tools
  • CMS Open Payments
  • FDA Orange Book
  • Medicare pricing files
  • matplotlib
data
CMS Open Payments general payments, 2022 to 2024, three GLP-1 brands · FDA Orange Book patents and exclusivity for semaglutide · 2025 wholesale acquisition cost and Medicare pricing · SURMOUNT-5, SELECT and STEP 8 trial results
status
finished
question

Zepbound beats Wegovy on the headline endpoint and costs less. Wegovy grew anyway. What was Novo actually selling, and where did the money go?

what i did
  • pulled every Open Payments record for Wegovy, Zepbound and Saxenda across 2022 to 2024 and split it by recipient specialty, so the question became where the money moved, not how big the total was.
  • traced the composition-of-matter patent through the Orange Book, the Hatch-Waxman extension and the Paragraph IV challenge to get the real selling window rather than the nominal patent term.
  • built the price stack from list down to what the patient actually pays each month, for both brands.
  • read the three head-to-head trials against each label to work out which claims each brand is allowed to make.
what came out
  • Wegovy's physician spend shifted from endocrinology to cardiology inside one year. Endocrinology fell from 66.6% of payment dollars to 33.8%; cardiovascular disease rose from 5.3% to 28.7%, with interventional cardiology taking another 8.4%. The SELECT indication landed in March 2024.
  • Novo raised its spend instead of ceding the ground. Wegovy's physician spend went from $0.29M to $4.54M to $6.86M across 2022 to 2024, still climbing in the year Zepbound scaled to $4.84M.
  • Saxenda was left to decline. Its spend collapsed from $4.42M to $0.13M as Novo moved behind the newer molecule.
  • Wegovy costs more and works less on the headline number: $1,349 list against Zepbound's $1,086, and 15 kg of weight loss at 72 weeks against 22.8 kg. The premium is not buying weight loss.
  • the patient pays about $135 a month, roughly $1,620 a year, for as long as they stay on it, and both labels imply that is indefinitely.
  • fifteen years ran between filing and approval. A 1,040-day Hatch-Waxman extension pushes expiry to December 2031, leaving about ten and a half years of selling, and Mylan's 2025 Delaware win shortens it again.
calls made
  • specialty share of payment dollars rather than payment counts. Counts are dominated by food and beverage items, which run about 97% of all records and say nothing about where the strategy went.
  • effective patent life measured from approval, not from filing, because the filing-to-approval gap is the part a commercial reader cannot get back.
  • list, net and patient-pay shown as one stack, since the gap between them is the actual negotiation and a single price hides it.
  • the positioning map is qualitative on both axes. The trade between the two brands is real; precise distances on a drawn chart would not be.
so what
  • a label expansion is a salesforce retargeting event. The SELECT indication moved roughly a third of the detailing budget into a specialty that had been a rounding error two years earlier. Anyone modelling the return on an outcomes trial should be costing the channel shift, not only the script lift.
  • being second on efficacy is survivable if you own a claim the other brand cannot make. Wegovy holds a price premium while losing the weight-loss comparison, because cardiovascular risk reduction is the only claim a cardiologist can act on.
  • the spending pattern shows the defence. An incumbent that raises physician investment in the year a superior rival scales is buying time on the exclusivity clock, and that clock is about ten years, not twenty.
  • chronic dosing with no defined endpoint turns a monthly copay into an adherence problem. The commercial risk here is discontinuation.
not shown
  • net prices after confidential rebates are not public; the discount here is a stated assumption, not an observed figure
