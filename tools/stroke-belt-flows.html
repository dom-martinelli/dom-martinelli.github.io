Where Stroke Belt patients go for a hospital stay

Each arc is Medicare inpatient stays from a patient's home county to the county of the hospital that treated them, across eight Stroke Belt states. Arcs shade from light at home to deep at the hospital. Dots are home counties, sized by how many stays leave.

What the map shows

Patients travel farther here. A Stroke Belt Medicare stay is a median 10.7 miles from home, against 7.3 in the median state outside the Belt, and 14% are more than 30 miles away (8% elsewhere). Mississippi is the extreme: 27% of stays are over 30 miles.

The hubs are the cities. Nashville, Atlanta, Birmingham, Charleston, Jackson, Knoxville, New Orleans and Little Rock each pull tens of thousands of stays from the counties around them.

Rings are rural hospitals that closed since 2010. The Belt has 48 of the country's 154. Arkansas and Louisiana, the two early Medicaid expanders here, had 2 between them; the six states that held out through the decade had 46, Tennessee alone 14. Across all states, a higher closure rate goes with a larger share of stays over 30 miles (Spearman ρ 0.41). These are associations, not a causal estimate.
